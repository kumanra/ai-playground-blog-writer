# Anti-AI Writing Instructions

Use this file as a writing guardrail.

Attach it to any AI chat, agent, writing workflow, Claude project, ChatGPT project, Cursor rule, AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md, or SKILLS.md file when you want the output to stop sounding like default AI writing.

This file is not an AI detector.
It does not prove whether a text was written by AI.
It is a practical editing rulebook for removing the common voice, structure, rhythm, and wording patterns that make writing feel machine-generated.

---

## 1. Main instruction

When writing, editing, rewriting, summarising, drafting, or polishing text:

Avoid the default AI writing voice.

Write like a person who knows what they want to say.
Do not write like a safe, polished, over-explaining assistant.
Do not fill space.
Do not make every idea sound important.
Do not turn ordinary points into grand claims.
Do not use shiny words when plain words work.
Do not make the writing smoother at the cost of meaning.

The goal is not to sound casual.
The goal is to sound real.

---

## 2. Core principle

AI writing usually fails in one way:

It sounds correct before it sounds true.

It often has:
- clean grammar
- balanced paragraphs
- confident claims
- polished transitions
- generic importance
- safe conclusions
- broad explanations
- very little friction
- very little original pressure

Human writing usually has more shape.
It has decisions.
It has emphasis.
It has missing symmetry.
It has a point of view.
It says some things and leaves other things out.
It does not treat every sentence like it has equal value.

---

## 3. How to use this file

When using this file with an AI assistant, start with this instruction:

```
Read the attached Anti-AI Writing Instructions file.
Apply it as a strict style and editing rule for everything you write in this chat.
Do not start writing yet.
First, understand my topic, audience, format, and intended voice.
Then write in a clear human style and avoid all AI writing patterns listed in the file.
```

For agents or coding assistants, add this to your project rules:

```
Before drafting any article, post, email, social message, landing page, documentation, or public text, read anti_ai_writing_instructions.md and apply it as a writing-quality rule.
```

## 4. What AI writing feels like

AI writing often feels:
- too smooth
- too balanced
- too polite
- too complete
- too eager to explain
- too eager to praise
- too eager to conclude
- too afraid of bluntness
- too dependent on structure
- too dependent on transitions
- too full of vague importance

The reader may not know exactly why it feels artificial.
But they can feel it.
It sounds like a text that was assembled, not lived through.

---

## 5. First rule: say the thing directly

Do not warm up for three sentences.
Do not open with a broad statement about the modern world.
Do not explain why the topic matters before saying what happened.

Bad:
> In today's fast-paced digital landscape, AI tools are transforming the way people create content.

Better:
> Most AI writing sounds the same because the model is trying to be safe.

Bad:
> Writing has always played a crucial role in how humans communicate ideas.

Better:
> The problem is not grammar. The problem is voice.

---

## 6. Do not use grand openings

Avoid openings like:
- In today's fast-paced world...
- In an era where...
- As technology continues to evolve...
- In the rapidly changing digital landscape...
- With the rise of AI...
- In the modern age...
- Throughout history...
- Now more than ever...
- As we move into the future...

These openings are usually filler.

Start closer to the actual point.
Use the second sentence first.

---

## 7. Do not restate the assignment

AI often begins by repeating the user's request in a polished way.

Bad:
> This article will explore how AI writing patterns can be identified and avoided in order to create more authentic content.

Better:
> You can usually spot AI writing before you can explain why it feels wrong.

Do not say what the piece will do unless the format truly requires it.
Just do it.

---

## 8. Avoid fake importance

AI loves to make every topic sound historic.
Watch for sentences that inflate ordinary facts into big meaning.

Avoid:
- This marks a pivotal moment...
- This represents a significant shift...
- This underscores the importance of...
- This highlights the need for...
- This serves as a testament to...
- This reflects a broader trend...
- This plays a crucial role in...
- This contributes to the evolving landscape of...
- This sets the stage for...
- This leaves an indelible mark...

Use specific consequences instead.

Bad:
> This update marks a pivotal moment in the evolution of AI productivity.

Better:
> This update matters because it lets the agent keep working without asking for the same context again.

Bad:
> The feature underscores the growing importance of memory in AI workflows.

Better:
> Without memory, the user has to explain the same thing every time.

---

## 9. Avoid surface-level analysis

AI often adds an analytical-sounding ending to a sentence even when it says nothing.

Watch for sentence endings like:
- ..., highlighting its importance.
- ..., reflecting a broader shift.
- ..., emphasizing the need for change.
- ..., showcasing its potential.
- ..., reinforcing its relevance.
- ..., contributing to future innovation.
- ..., ensuring a better user experience.
- ..., fostering deeper engagement.

These endings usually pretend to explain.
Cut them or replace them with something concrete.

Bad:
> The new workflow stores project rules, highlighting the importance of context in agentic systems.

Better:
> The new workflow stores project rules, so the agent does not need to be re-taught the same preferences every session.

Bad:
> The guide gives users valuable insights into AI writing, helping them navigate the complexities of modern content creation.

Better:
> The guide shows which words, openings, and sentence shapes make writing sound like a chatbot.

---

## 10. Use specific details instead of broad claims

AI writing often replaces the real detail with a generic summary.

Bad:
> The tool improves productivity across different workflows.

Better:
> The tool can read a folder, follow the project rules, rewrite the draft, and leave a short change log.

Bad:
> The article received strong engagement from readers.

Better:
> The article brought 1,200 new subscribers in two days.

Bad:
> The company has built a robust platform for creators.

Better:
> The company lets creators publish newsletters, charge subscriptions, and send posts by email.

Specific beats impressive.
A small real detail is better than a big vague sentence.

---

## 11. Avoid the AI vocabulary cloud

Do not use these words unless they are necessary, quoted, or part of a proper name.

### Strongly avoid
delve, dive into, navigate, underscore, bolster, foster, harness, leverage, unpack, shed light on, pave the way, pivotal, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, game-changing, transformative, innovative, robust, comprehensive, seamless, intricate, nuanced, vibrant, multifaceted, holistic, testament, landscape, realm, tapestry, interplay, meticulous, meticulously, enduring, valuable insights, rich history, rich cultural heritage, natural beauty, nestled, in the heart of, showcase, boasts, features (when you mean has), offers (when you mean has), stands as, serves as, marks a shift, plays a crucial role

### Use with care
important, significant, key, critical, major, powerful, interesting, useful, clear, meaningful, impactful, high-quality, engaging, impressive, advanced, better, new, modern, future

These words are not banned.
But they are lazy when they replace the actual point.

Do not say something is important.
Show why it matters.

## 12. Avoid AI phrase patterns

Do not use these common AI phrase shapes:
- It is important to note that...
- It is worth noting that...
- At its core...
- At the end of the day...
- When it comes to...
- This is where X comes in.
- Let's break it down.
- Let's explore.
- Let's dive in.
- In this guide, we will explore...
- In conclusion...
- In summary...
- Ultimately...
- Despite these challenges...
- The future of X lies in...
- X is more than just Y.
- X is not just Y. It is Z.
- Not only X, but also Y.
- No X. No Y. Just Z.
- From X to Y, ...
- Whether X or Y, ...
- By doing X, users can Y.
- This enables users to...
- This empowers users to...
- This helps users navigate...
- This creates a more seamless experience.

These forms are not always wrong.
But AI uses them too often.
If one appears in a draft, treat it as suspicious and rewrite it.

---

## 13. Avoid fake contrast

AI likes contrast that looks smart but says little.

Bad:
> This is not just a writing problem. It is a thinking problem.

This can work sometimes, but AI overuses it.
Use it only when the contrast is sharp and needed.

Better:
> Bad AI writing usually starts before the first sentence. The model has no real decision to make, so it fills the page with safe language.

Bad:
> Claude Code is not just a coding assistant. It is a new way to work with software.

Better:
> Claude Code becomes useful when it can read your files, follow your rules, edit code, test changes, and keep track of what it already tried.

Concrete function beats dramatic contrast.

---

## 14. Avoid the rule of three when it becomes a habit

AI loves groups of three.

It writes:
- simple, powerful, and effective
- fast, flexible, and reliable
- creativity, productivity, and efficiency
- identity, authenticity, and trust

One group of three is fine.
A whole article full of triplets sounds artificial.

Break the rhythm.
Use one word.
Use two.
Use four if the list really has four.
Do not force three because it sounds complete.

Bad:
> This workflow is simple, practical, and powerful.

Better:
> This workflow is simple enough to use every day.

---

## 15. Avoid uniform paragraph rhythm

AI often writes paragraphs that are almost the same length.
It creates a smooth wall.

Human writing has pressure.
Some paragraphs are one line.
Some carry more detail.
Some sentences stop early.
Some explain.

Use rhythm like thinking, not like a template.

Bad rhythm:
> AI writing often follows predictable patterns that make it easy to identify. These patterns include repeated sentence structures, generic vocabulary, and a lack of personal voice. By understanding these signs, writers can improve their work and create more authentic content.
>
> Human writing, by contrast, tends to include more variation and specificity. It may contain uneven rhythm, sharper claims, and more personal judgment. This makes it feel more natural and trustworthy to readers.

Better rhythm:
> AI writing is easy to read.
>
> That is part of the problem.
>
> Every sentence arrives polished. Every paragraph sits neatly beside the next one. Nothing feels argued with.
>
> Human writing usually has more pressure in it. A sentence gets shorter because the point matters. A paragraph gets longer because the detail needs room.

---

## 16. Avoid over-formatting

AI often overuses formatting to make weak content look organised.

Watch for:
- too many headings
- too many bullet points
- too many numbered lists
- too much bold text
- bold label followed by explanation
- tables that do not need to be tables
- title case headings everywhere
- generic sections like Overview, Key Benefits, Challenges, Future Outlook, Conclusion

Avoid this pattern:
```
- **Efficiency:** The tool helps users save time.
- **Flexibility:** The tool works across workflows.
- **Scalability:** The tool supports future growth.
```

Better:
> The tool saves time in one boring place: repeat setup.
>
> You do not have to paste the same rules into every new chat.

Use formatting only when it helps the reader.
Do not use formatting to fake structure.

---

## 17. Avoid generic conclusions

AI likes to end with a neat bow.

Avoid endings like:
- In conclusion, AI writing is a powerful tool, but it must be used responsibly.
- Ultimately, the future of writing depends on balancing efficiency with authenticity.
- As technology continues to evolve, writers must adapt while preserving the human touch.
- By understanding these patterns, creators can produce content that is both engaging and meaningful.

End on substance.
End with a useful point, a sharp warning, a practical next step, or a sentence that actually belongs to the piece.

Bad:
> Ultimately, avoiding AI writing patterns is essential for creating authentic and meaningful content in the digital age.

Better:
> If the sentence could appear in a thousand other articles, cut it.

---

## 18. Do not fake emotion

AI sometimes tries to sound human by adding warmth, drama, or soft emotion.

Avoid fake human lines like:
- This is where things get exciting.
- This feels almost magical.
- This is a beautiful reminder that...
- There is something deeply human about...
- It is both inspiring and humbling.

Do not simulate feelings.
Use emotion only when it comes from a real situation, real stakes, or the user's actual voice.

Bad:
> There is something deeply human about wanting our words to feel authentic.

Better:
> Readers can tell when a sentence was written to sound nice instead of to say something.

---

## 19. Do not invent personal experience

Never add fake anecdotes.
Never pretend the writer tested something if they did not.

Never write:
- I tried this for a week...
- In my experience...
- I have seen this happen many times...
- When I first discovered this...

unless the user provided that experience or clearly asked for a fictional example.

Human writing does not mean fake diary writing.
Real voice does not require fake confession.

---

## 20. Avoid vague authority

AI often makes claims sound supported without naming a real source.

Avoid:
- Experts say...
- Many believe...
- Some argue...
- Observers note...
- Industry reports suggest...
- Researchers have found...
- It has been widely recognised...
- Critics argue...
- Studies show...

Use a named source or cut the claim.

Bad:
> Experts say AI writing is becoming harder to detect.

Better:
> The Atlantic argued in May 2026 that AI writing can trick readers when they skim, but starts to feel hollow when read closely.

Bad:
> Many creators are now using AI to improve their writing workflow.

Better:
> A creator can use AI for outlining, editing, and checking structure without letting it write the final voice.

If you cannot name who said it, rewrite the sentence as your own observation or remove it.

---

## 21. Avoid name-dropping as proof

AI often lists famous outlets, companies, people, or institutions to create credibility.

Bad:
> The idea has been discussed by writers from The Atlantic, Wikipedia, LinkedIn, GitHub, and leading AI communities.

Better:
> Wikipedia's AI-writing guide is useful because it lists the repeated sentence shapes editors keep seeing in pasted AI text.

One specific source used well is better than five names stacked together.

---

## 22. Avoid false ranges

AI often writes fake ranges with "from X to Y."

Bad:
> AI writing appears in everything from emails to essays.

That may be fine if the range is real.
But AI overuses it.

Worse:
> The guide covers everything from tone to authenticity.

That is not a useful range.
Be exact.

Better:
> The guide covers openings, word choice, sentence rhythm, formatting, sourcing, and conclusions.

---

## 23. Avoid unnecessary hedge words

AI often sounds safe because it hedges too much.

Watch for:
may, might, could, can help, can be seen as, often, generally, somewhat, arguably, in many cases, tends to, potentially, relatively, appears to, suggests that

Hedging is useful when the claim is uncertain.
But do not hedge obvious points.

Bad:
> AI writing can sometimes feel somewhat generic.

Better:
> AI writing often feels generic.

Bad:
> This may help users create more authentic content.

Better:
> This helps writers remove the phrases that make a draft sound machine-written.

---

## 24. Avoid over-politeness

AI often writes like it is afraid to offend anyone.

Avoid:
- Great question.
- Absolutely.
- Certainly.
- I hope this helps.
- Feel free to reach out.
- I would be happy to assist.
- Thank you for sharing.
- Your thoughtful question raises an important point.

Use only when the situation needs it.
Most writing should begin with the answer.

---

## 25. Avoid assistant voice in public writing

Do not leave chatbot residue in the draft.

Remove:
- Here is a revised version.
- Below is the article.
- Certainly.
- Of course.
- As requested.
- I can help with that.
- Let me know if you want me to...
- Would you like me to...

The published text should not sound like it came out of a chat window.

---

## 26. Avoid knowledge-cutoff language

Do not write like a model explaining its limits unless the context requires it.

Avoid:
- As of my last update...
- Based on available information...
- The sources do not provide enough detail...
- Publicly available data suggests...
- Up to my knowledge cutoff...

If a fact is uncertain, say it naturally.

Better:
> I could not verify the exact number.

or:
> The company has not published the full details yet.

---

## 27. Avoid bland positivity

AI is trained to sound helpful, so it often becomes too positive.

Watch for:
exciting, powerful, impressive, remarkable, promising, valuable, meaningful, incredible, beautiful, transformative

Do not praise unless the detail earns it.

Bad:
> This powerful new workflow unlocks incredible productivity.

Better:
> This workflow saves time because the model reads the project rules before it starts writing.

---

## 28. Avoid promotional tone

Do not make normal products, people, places, or tools sound like ads.

Avoid:
boasts, world-class, renowned, leading, premium, exceptional, state-of-the-art, one-of-a-kind, unmatched, next-generation, beautifully designed, dynamic hub, rich tapestry, vibrant community, breathtaking, nestled in

Bad:
> Substack is a vibrant platform that empowers creators to build meaningful relationships with their audiences.

Better:
> Substack lets writers publish posts, send them by email, and charge readers directly.

---

## 29. Avoid fake neutrality

AI often sounds neutral by making both sides equally vague.

Bad:
> While AI writing offers many benefits, it also raises important concerns about authenticity and originality.

Better:
> AI is useful for structure and editing. It becomes a problem when it replaces the writer's own thinking.

Bad:
> There are both opportunities and challenges.

Better:
> The opportunity is speed. The risk is that every writer starts sounding like the same assistant.

---

## 30. Avoid "both sides" endings

AI often ends by balancing everything.

Avoid:
- The key is to strike a balance.
- Writers must balance efficiency with authenticity.
- The future will depend on responsible use.
- It is important to use AI as a tool, not a replacement.

These can be true, but they are usually too generic.
Make the ending sharper.

Better:
> Use AI to clean the draft. Do not let it remove the fingerprints.

---

## 31. Avoid empty "human touch" language

AI often says writing needs a human touch.
That phrase is now part of the AI voice.

Avoid:
human touch, human element, authentic connection, genuine emotion, emotional depth, heart and soul, truly engaging narratives

Say what the human actually adds.

Better:
> A human writer chooses what to leave rough, what to cut, and what to say without softening it.

---

## 32. Prefer verbs that show action

Replace abstract verbs with real ones.

Instead of: enhances, empowers, enables, facilitates, fosters, cultivates, showcases, highlights, underscores, reflects, represents

Use: shows, makes, cuts, adds, removes, keeps, saves, changes, checks, stores, writes, reads, asks, finds, fixes

Bad:
> This file empowers users to create more authentic writing.

Better:
> This file tells the model which habits to avoid before it writes.

---

## 33. Prefer nouns people can picture

AI writing leans on abstract nouns.

Watch for: innovation, transformation, authenticity, engagement, complexity, efficiency, productivity, creativity, connection, landscape, ecosystem, framework, paradigm

These words are not banned.
But use them only when needed.
A real object, action, file, screen, number, mistake, prompt, reader, or sentence is usually stronger.

Bad:
> This improves content authenticity.

Better:
> This removes the openings and phrases that make the draft sound copied from ChatGPT.

---

## 34. Watch sentence beginnings

AI repeats sentence starters.

Watch for too many sentences beginning with:
This means..., This allows..., This helps..., This creates..., This ensures..., By doing this..., As a result..., Additionally..., Moreover..., Furthermore..., However..., In contrast..., For example...

Use transitions when needed.
But do not let them drive the writing.
Often the sentence works better without them.

Bad:
> Additionally, this file helps users avoid common AI writing patterns.

Better:
> The file also catches common AI writing patterns.

Even better:
> The file catches the habits most people miss.

---

## 35. Reduce transition words

AI over-connects ideas.
Human writing can trust the reader more.

Cut extra transitions like:
furthermore, moreover, additionally, in addition, therefore, thus, consequently, nevertheless, on the other hand, in contrast, as a result

Use simple transitions: but, so, also, still, because, now, then

Or use no transition.

---

## 36. Avoid elegant variation

AI sometimes tries too hard not to repeat a word.
It uses synonyms that make the text feel unnatural.

Bad:
> The article explains AI writing. The piece also shows how machine-generated prose affects readers. This content then explores how automated language can be improved.

Better:
> The article explains AI writing. It shows why the same phrases keep appearing and how to remove them.

Repeat the normal word if it is the right word.
Do not vary language just to look polished.

---

## 37. Avoid synthetic chattiness

AI sometimes tries to sound casual in a fake way.

Avoid:
- Let's be honest.
- Here's the thing.
- You know what?
- Sounds simple, right?
- Pretty cool, right?
- And that's the magic.
- Spoiler: it works.

Use casual language only if it fits the writer.
Real casual writing is specific, not performative.

---

## 38. Avoid perfect symmetry

AI likes balance.
It writes two-part and three-part structures too often.

Bad:
> It saves time, reduces effort, and improves quality.

Better:
> It saves time because the writer does not have to keep removing the same phrases by hand.

Bad:
> The tool is simple enough for beginners and powerful enough for professionals.

Better:
> A beginner can use it as a checklist. A working writer can use it as a cleanup pass before publishing.

---

## 39. Avoid "safe classroom essay" tone

AI often writes like a student trying to satisfy the rubric.

Signs:
- clear thesis but no pressure
- repeated central idea
- tidy paragraphs
- obvious topic sentences
- conclusion that restates everything
- no surprising detail
- no real disagreement
- no sharp edit

Do not write to prove you understood the topic.
Write to say something useful.

---

## 40. Avoid repeating the same insight

AI often says the same thing three times:
1. states the point
2. explains the point
3. says why the point matters
4. restates the point in the conclusion

Do not do this.
Say the point once.
Then move.

Bad:
> AI writing patterns are important to understand because they affect how readers perceive authenticity. These patterns can make text feel less personal and more machine-generated. By identifying these patterns, writers can produce more authentic work that feels more human.

Better:
> AI writing often feels fake because it keeps choosing the safest possible sentence.

---

## 41. Do not over-explain obvious ideas

AI explains things the reader already knows.

Bad:
> Writing is a form of communication that allows people to share ideas, emotions, and information.

Better:
> A draft can be grammatically correct and still feel dead.

Before explaining, ask: Does the reader need this?
If not, cut it.

---

## 42. Use examples that feel real

AI examples are often generic.

Bad:
> For example, a business owner can use AI to improve their workflow.

Better:
> A newsletter writer can ask the model to remove em dashes, cut grand openings, and replace vague claims with exact examples from the draft.

Bad:
> A student can use this to improve assignments.

Better:
> A student can use this to remove the paragraph that says the topic is "increasingly important in today's world" and start with the actual argument instead.

---

## 43. Keep claims checkable

AI often writes claims that sound true but cannot be checked.

Bad:
> This method is widely used by successful creators.

Better:
> This method works best when you already know the point you want to make.

Bad:
> Readers increasingly prefer authentic content.

Better:
> Readers notice when every paragraph has the same polished rhythm.

---

## 44. Do not imitate "thought leadership" voice

Avoid LinkedIn-style certainty.

Bad:
> The future belongs to those who master AI without losing their humanity.

Better:
> The advantage is simple: use AI for the boring parts, then edit until the writing sounds like you again.

Bad:
> AI is redefining the boundaries of creativity.

Better:
> AI can give you a draft. It cannot decide what you actually believe.

---

## 45. Do not use fake depth

AI often writes lines that sound deep but collapse when read twice.

Bad:
> It fills the silence between technology and humanity.

Bad:
> It reflects the quiet tension between efficiency and authenticity.

Bad:
> It invites us to reconsider what it means to create in a machine-shaped world.

These lines feel meaningful because they use soft abstract words.
Replace them with a claim that can be argued with.

Better:
> If AI writes the first draft and the final edit, the writer may never do the thinking that makes the piece worth reading.

---

## 46. Do not remove all personality

Cleaning AI writing does not mean making text flat.

Keep: opinion, judgment, small details, directness, uneven rhythm, real examples, the writer's preferred words, the writer's natural sentence length

Remove: filler, fake warmth, fake importance, template structure, broad claims, repeated transitions, polished vagueness

The goal is not plainness alone.
The goal is controlled voice.

---

## 47. Do not add random imperfections

Do not add typos to look human.
Do not add slang if the writer does not use slang.
Do not add awkward grammar on purpose.
Do not add fake confusion.

Human writing is not bad writing.
Human writing is writing with decisions behind it.

---

## 48. Voice rules for newsletter writing

For newsletter or Substack-style writing:
- Start with the actual idea.
- Use short paragraphs.
- Keep the tone practical.
- Do not sound like a press release.
- Do not over-teach obvious background.
- Give the reader something they can use.
- Use examples from real workflows.
- Do not turn the article into a generic explainer.
- Do not end with "the future of..." language.
- Keep the author's opinion visible.

Bad:
> In today's fast-paced creator economy, newsletters have emerged as a powerful way to build meaningful audience relationships.

Better:
> A newsletter works when the reader feels the writer actually noticed something useful.

---

## 49. Voice rules for social posts

For social posts:
- Do not over-polish.
- Do not use fake viral hooks.
- Do not write like a LinkedIn carousel.
- Do not force every line to sound dramatic.
- Use one clear point.
- Make the first line specific.
- Leave some air.
- Avoid inspirational endings.

Bad:
> AI is not just changing how we write. It is changing how we think, create, and connect.

Better:
> The easiest way to spot AI writing is not the em dash.
>
> It is the lack of a real decision.
>
> Every sentence sounds equally safe.

---

## 50. Voice rules for emails

For emails:
- Be clear.
- Be polite without sounding automated.
- Do not over-explain.
- Do not use "I hope this email finds you well" unless the user wants it.
- Do not use corporate filler.
- Ask directly.
- End naturally.

Bad:
> I am reaching out to inquire about the possibility of obtaining further information regarding your services.

Better:
> I'm writing to ask for a quote for commercial garbage and recycling pickup.

---

## 51. Voice rules for technical guides

For technical guides:
- Give the step.
- Explain why it matters only when useful.
- Use exact filenames, commands, and folder names.
- Do not over-sell the method.
- Do not describe the tool as powerful unless you show the action.
- Prefer a working example over a general benefit.

Bad:
> This setup empowers developers to streamline their workflow and enhance productivity.

Better:
> Put this file in the project root so the agent reads the writing rules before it drafts anything.

---

## 52. Voice rules for academic writing

For academic writing:
- Keep the argument clear.
- Do not add fake complexity.
- Do not use "nuanced" as a decoration.
- Do not overuse "underscores," "highlights," or "reflects."
- Use named theorists, texts, policies, or examples.
- Explain the link, do not announce the significance.

Bad:
> This underscores the importance of leadership in navigating complex organizational landscapes.

Better:
> This shows that leadership decisions are shaped by informal pressure, not only by formal authority.

---

## 53. Rewrite protocol

When editing AI-sounding writing, follow this order.

**Step 1: Find the real point**
Ask: What is this paragraph actually saying?
Write that in one plain sentence.

**Step 2: Cut the throat-clearing**
Remove the opening phrase if it only sets the scene.
Delete: In today's world..., As technology evolves..., It is important to note..., When it comes to...

**Step 3: Replace vague praise with function**
Ask: What does this actually do?
Change: "This powerful tool enhances productivity."
To: "This tool saves the draft rules so the writer does not repeat them in every chat."

**Step 4: Replace abstract nouns with visible details**
Change: authenticity, engagement, innovation, efficiency, transformation
Into something the reader can see, do, count, open, edit, or test.

**Step 5: Break the rhythm**
Vary the sentence length.
Cut repeated structure.
Do not make every paragraph the same size.

**Step 6: Remove the generic ending**
If the ending could fit almost any article about AI, cut it.
End with the strongest useful sentence.

## 54. Detection checklist

Before publishing, scan the draft for these signs.

**Opening**
- Does it start with a broad statement about the modern world?
- Does it say "In today's..."?
- Does it restate the topic instead of making a point?
- Could the first paragraph fit 100 other articles?

**Words**
- Are there AI vocabulary words like "delve," "pivotal," "robust," "seamless," "landscape," or "underscore"?
- Are there too many abstract nouns?
- Are simple verbs replaced with inflated verbs?
- Is the text praising without proof?

**Structure**
- Are paragraphs the same length?
- Are there too many lists?
- Is there a repeated "Bold term: explanation" pattern?
- Are headings generic?
- Is there too much title case?

**Sentences**
- Are there too many em dashes?
- Are there too many triplets?
- Are there too many "not just X, but Y" contrasts?
- Are sentence endings pretending to analyse with "highlighting," "reflecting," or "underscoring"?

**Meaning**
- Does each paragraph add something new?
- Is the draft saying the same thing in different words?
- Are claims specific enough to check?
- Are sources named when the text implies outside authority?

**Ending**
- Does it start with "In conclusion," "Ultimately," or "In summary"?
- Does it end with "balance," "human touch," or "future of" language?
- Could the ending be pasted under almost any AI article?

If yes, rewrite.

---

## 55. Final editing test

Read the draft out loud.
Mark every sentence that sounds like:
- a press release
- a corporate blog
- a school essay
- a LinkedIn post
- a chatbot response
- a generic explainer
- a motivational ending

Rewrite those sentences first.

Then ask: Where is the real thought?

If the draft is smooth but empty, add thought.
If the draft is long but repetitive, cut.
If the draft is clear but generic, add a specific detail.
If the draft is correct but lifeless, make a choice.

## 58. Strict banned structures

Avoid these sentence structures:
- It is not just X, it is Y.
- X is more than just Y.
- Not only does X do Y, but it also does Z.
- From X to Y, Z is reshaping A.
- Whether X or Y, Z offers something for everyone.
- By combining X with Y, users can Z.
- Despite its many benefits, X faces several challenges.
- As X continues to evolve, Y will become increasingly important.

Rewrite with direct meaning.

## 59. Strong replacement habits

**Replace "important" with the reason**
Bad: "This is important for writers."
Better: "This stops the model from adding the same polished filler to every draft."

**Replace "powerful" with the action**
Bad: "This is a powerful workflow."
Better: "The workflow checks the draft against a list of known AI patterns before you publish."

**Replace "seamless" with what becomes easier**
Bad: "It creates a seamless writing process."
Better: "You keep the rule file in the project, so you do not paste the same style instructions every time."

**Replace "transformative" with the before and after**
Bad: "This is a transformative change for creators."
Better: "Before, the writer had to manually remove AI phrases. Now the agent checks for them before drafting."

- overused AI words

- rule-of-three habits
- excessive transitions

## 64. Editorial standard

The final text should feel like:
- someone had a reason to write it
- someone made choices
- someone cut the filler
- someone knew the reader's problem
- someone used AI as an assistant, not as the author

The final text should not feel like:
- a safe explainer
- a search result summary
- a corporate blog
- a school essay
- a LinkedIn carousel
- a press release
- a chatbot answer pasted into public

If a sentence sounds like it could appear in any AI-generated article on the same topic, rewrite it.
